# First playable release

## Player loop

Players enter one shared, persistent world at a protected settlement plot. They gather **ore**, place a **smelter**, turn ore into **ingots**, research metallurgy, and use a **workshop** to forge **tools** for territorial expansion. Masonry then opens a second raw material: a **quarry** cuts **stone** out of the mountain ranges, a **brickworks** fires stone and timber into **brick**, and brick pays for **walls** and for a permanent second tier on the buildings a settlement already relies on. A **Civic Charter** opens a second age on top of both chains, where a **foundry** casts **steel** from ingots and brick for bastions, guild halls, and the world's shared projects. They cooperatively repair their buildings when a small environmental hazard damages them, and contribute to a rotating world project that never runs out. The first slice is deliberately limited to prove the authoritative command, simulation, rendering, and synchronization paths.

## Fixed decisions

- The world is procedurally generated and conceptually unbounded. Coordinates are signed 32-bit tile coordinates; generation is deterministic from a world seed.
- Tiles are diamond isometric tiles, 64 by 32 screen pixels. Chunks are 16 by 16 tiles; buildings may occupy at most 2 by 2 tiles.
- Terrain has height, and only mountains carry it. Ridged fractal Perlin noise raises 13-15% of tiles to levels 1-5 and marks them `mountain`, as connected ranges that climb from foothills to a summit with about two thirds of those tiles above level one; every other tile, including every resource deposit, stays at level zero. Mountains are impassable and unbuildable, so construction, logistics, population, research, and combat all continue to work on flat ground while ranges give the world relief and block movement. Elevation is a pure function of the world seed, and the server sends it per explored tile alongside terrain so unexplored relief cannot be reconstructed. A starter plot must be at least 87.5% open ground, so a new player is never walled in.
- Logistics uses internal building inventories plus deterministic, recipe-validated links, and every link is served by one carrier that walks. Each link has player-set minimum and maximum target stock: it dispatches below the minimum, refills only to the maximum, and retains bounded recent and lifetime delivery totals. Completed storage, extractors, and producers send inputs into completed producers or storage buffers; storage-to-storage links are rejected because they would only shuffle items between buffers. A carrier loads up to a fixed per-trip capacity, walks the link's route tile by tile, unloads what the target has room for, and walks home before the link can dispatch again, so a link's real throughput is set by distance rather than by a flat per-tick rate. Priority ordering reserves target capacity per tick, preserves output space for active batches, and reports congestion, recipe reconfiguration, satisfied stock, or a route no open ground connects. Routes are planned once from the world seed and the two tiles, over open ground only, so a range between two buildings forces a detour. Paving a route halves the movement each step costs and Engineering research raises the per-tick movement budget and carrier capacity. Items in flight belong to the carrier and to nothing else: they leave the source when it loads and reappear only when it unloads, and demolishing or unlinking hands them back rather than destroying them. Belts and multi-carrier links remain future work.
- Population uses an aggregated-worker model. A settlement starts with two settlers; completed housing increases its capacity, settlers arrive gradually when capacity is available, and active producers consume deterministic worker slots according to player-set priorities. Satisfaction is derived from shelter, assigned work, and a completed hearth service. Every 200 ticks, healthy settlements gain a settler if they have room; unsatisfied settlements above their protected starting population lose one. Employment, available-worker counts, and actionable housing/work/wellbeing alerts are visible in the HUD. Individual home-to-work walking is deliberately not simulated in the first release; construction-worker ticks and material-delivery state represent required travel and transport, while scouts and threats use explicit bounded paths.
- Every 8 by 8 terrain sector has one deterministic, finite ore deposit and one finite timber grove, and every mountain tile is a finite stone deposit. The initial chain is ore -> ingot in a smelter, then ingot + wood -> tool in a metallurgy-locked workshop. Tools pay for Territorial Charters. Each player starts with enough wood to build one smelter and can gather more from timber groves.
- Stone is quarried out of the ranges that already shape the map rather than from a new terrain type, so relief is a resource as well as an obstacle and no existing tile moves. Mountains stay impassable and unbuildable: a quarry is placed on open ground within four tiles of a range, exactly like a mine beside an ore deposit. Because a starter plot is nearly clear ground, reaching stone usually means claiming a sector toward a range, which makes mountain-adjacent territory worth having. The masonry chain is stone + wood -> brick in a brickworks, and brick pays for walls and for building tiers.
- Masonry sits outside the permanent Engineering/Stewardship choice. It gates the quarry, the brickworks, the wall, and every building tier, so making it compete with a one-way development branch would turn that choice into a dead end.
- Buildings have a permanent tier. Upgrading a smelter, workshop, brickworks, mine, lumber camp, quarry, or storage re-enters the ordinary construction pipeline: the building stops working, a builder has to deliver the tier's tools and brick, and on completion it comes out faster, roomier, tougher, and at full health. A tier is therefore paid for in downtime as well as materials. An upgrade waits for a running batch rather than discarding inputs that were already consumed, is charged to the building owner's stock so a delegated builder spends the settlement's materials rather than their own, and can be cancelled for a full refund, which leaves the tier-one building working instead of removing it.
- Raw extraction is automatable. After one immediate manual gather, a player may queue up to twenty units from that deposit; one settler gathers the remainder every three ticks and the order may be cancelled. A mine, lumber camp, or quarry placed within four tiles of a deposit that still has yield takes one item every few ticks into its own inventory while a settler is assigned to it. Both paths draw from the same finite deposits and starting-plot reservations, occupy the shared worker pool, and conserve every item.
- A second age follows the first. The **Civic Charter** needs both first-age chains behind it — the Territorial Charter and Masonry — and opens **Metalcasting**, **Fortification**, and the guild hall. A **foundry** casts **steel** from ingots and brick together, so the age cannot be entered on one chain alone: a settlement finishes both or trades for what it lacks. Steel pays for a **bastion**, which answers a raid where a watchtower answers one raider, for the foundry's own tier, and for the world's steel project. Metalcasting then offers the age's permanent choice, and like the first one it splits a recipe rather than gating a building: **Precision Casting** spends brick where the base recipe spends ingots and is quicker, **Bulk Casting** is the cheapest steel per unit and the slowest to arrive. Neither closes the foundry, because steel is what a shared project asks for. Settler satisfaction counts one service of each kind, so a guild hall is worth building beside a hearth and worth nothing twice over, and every defence building now contributes its own strength rather than the watchtower's.
- The world's shared projects rotate and repeat instead of ending. One is open at a time — the Frontier Beacon asks for tools, the Great Causeway for brick, the Grand Foundry for steel — and each rewards the currency of the age below it, so a finished project pays back into the chain the next one needs. A completed project stays open for a bounded claim window and is then retired by the clock: its live totals reset, its bounded audit trail survives, and the next project opens. The order wraps, and every completed lap raises each project's target by a fixed amount up to a ceiling, so a permanent world keeps shared work at a height its players can actually reach. Unclaimed rewards expire with the window rather than becoming a standing obligation the world has to remember. Which project is open, and how high its bar stands, both follow from one counter of completed projects, so the rotation cannot drift from the content table or disagree with a replay.
- Landmark discoveries wait for a permanent salvage-or-develop decision. Salvage grants a bounded immediate resource reward. Developing ruins accelerates future research, preserving fertile groves improves local forestry, and surveying mountain passes improves carrier movement.
- Tools and bricks remain useful after one-time research and upgrades through repeatable five-minute settlement initiatives. A Freight Charter improves carrier movement and capacity; a Builders' Festival doubles construction work per tick. Only one initiative may be active per player.
- Engineering and Stewardship are complementary permanent specializations. Engineering improves transport and unlocks an ingot-only two-tool recipe; Stewardship unlocks renewable forestry and a wood-efficient two-tool recipe, encouraging trade between differently specialized settlements.
- New players receive a non-overlapping 8 by 8 protected plot on a deterministic spiral around the origin. Owners may build only in their own plot. Every player starts as the owner of a private settlement; owners can invite members, who must accept, revoke membership, and transfer settlement ownership to a current member before leaving. Builders may configure, repair, and deconstruct shared buildings; logistics members may load and withdraw items; ordinary members have no building authority. Direct resource transfers are atomic and retained as participant-visible history.
- Exploration is private until players explicitly cooperate. The server sends each client terrain only for its explored chunks and never sends the deterministic world seed. Territory uses 8 by 8 tile sectors: a player may claim an explored sector adjacent to their existing territory after researching the Territorial Charter. Claimed sectors are public; building inventories and unexplored sectors are private.
- Inactive settlements remain in the world. Simulation runs at the same fidelity for all loaded state in the first slice.
- On planned downtime, simulation pauses at a completed checkpoint. No offline production is granted.
- The first threat is a repairable acid-rain event that damages an owned building; it never targets players or enables PvP.
- The first active PvE threat is a deterministic raider swarm. It spawns away from its selected completed non-center building, navigates through passable terrain, and only damages that fixed target once adjacent. Nearby watchtowers engage it automatically; it never accepts player-issued attack targets, retargets onto a neighbor, or routes through another settlement's protected plot.
- New settlements receive 300 ticks of hazard and raid protection. After 300 ticks without an accepted command, a settlement is treated as inactive: environmental and raider damage can still reduce buildings to 50% health, and at most one raider may pressure that settlement, but unattended damage cannot destroy it. An accepted command immediately makes the settlement active again, so disconnecting is not an instant shield and returning resumes full risk.
- Inactive regions continue through the same deterministic simulation pipeline as observed regions. Level-of-detail simulation is disabled unless a future design and replay test can prove equivalent outcomes; reduced observation must never change production, logistics, hazards, research, or combat results.
- Account deletion is an irreversible authoritative transaction. Players must first transfer every settlement they own; deletion then removes their private simulation state, owned buildings and scouts, land claims, memberships, invitations, block metadata, and display name. Historical transaction, contribution, chat, and moderation records remain bounded and server-side where required for integrity, with chat display-name snapshots replaced. A private tombstone permanently reserves the deleted player identifier and is never sent to clients.
- Administrative investigation uses scoped world, player, settlement, transaction, moderation, and audit inspection rather than direct database editing. Corrections are limited to inventory restoration, settlement-owner repair, retained-message removal, and report resolution. Every accepted correction validates the whole resulting world and atomically stores a completed checkpoint with an immutable actor, reason, operation, target, before/after state, and tick record.
- Commands that overlap while persistence is awaiting I/O are applied through one authoritative queue. Transfer capacity, duplicate retries, role revocation, membership acceptance/removal, and ownership transfer/removal resolve in queue order: each command validates the committed result before it, resources remain conserved, and every settlement retains exactly one owner.
- Every settlement keeps a one-tile westward access lane from its center and a two-tile foreign ownership buffer. Territory claims cannot surround that buffer, buildings cannot block the access lane, and gathering in overlapping starting areas is reserved for the nearest starting plot (with player ID as a deterministic tie-breaker).
- The first global cooperative objective is the Frontier Beacon. Separate settlements contribute a bounded total of tools; contribution commands and settlement/player totals are recorded authoritatively. Completion occurs at an exact deterministic threshold. Every contributor may claim one fixed ingot reward, with the claim command recorded permanently and retries rejected. Clients see global progress and only their own contributor identity and history.
- Settlement owners and builders may reserve up to three shared construction projects on valid settlement land. Any current member can contribute the exact remaining materials. The server retains contributor, item, amount, command, and tick history; once fully funded it creates one building owned by the settlement owner and duplicate retries cannot contribute twice. Project coordinates and contributor history are visible only to relevant settlement members.
- Player and settlement display names are normalized with Unicode NFKC and collapsed whitespace, have bounded type-specific lengths, and are unique under case-insensitive comparison within their respective namespaces. The server applies a configurable moderated-term list before accepting a name.
- The public world directory supports bounded, paginated searches for player and settlement identifiers or display names. Results disclose only names and player IDs, or a settlement's name, ID, owner ID, and member count—never coordinates, territory, inventories, roles, invitations, activity, or hidden-world entities. Directory results can populate resource-transfer and invitation controls.
- Global and settlement chat messages are normalized, length-limited, content-filtered, retained within a fixed bound, and subject to a persisted five-tick sender cooldown. Settlement messages reach only current members. A player's private block list filters prior and future messages from blocked senders, while reports preserve a server-side message snapshot without exposing report metadata to clients.
- Development worlds may be reset freely. Persistent alpha, beta, and public worlds require an announced reset policy.

## Non-goals

This increment does not include production accounts, combat, belt logistics, trade offers, or mobile support. A wall blocks raider routes because every building does; there is no dedicated fortification, siege, or gate rule. Elevation applies to mountains only: no playable tile is raised, and there is no ramp, bridge, or line-of-sight rule. It targets current desktop Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari at a minimum 1024 by 768 viewport.

# First playable release

## Player loop

Players enter one shared, persistent world at a protected settlement plot. They gather **ore**, place a **smelter**, turn ore into **ingots**, research metallurgy, and use a **workshop** to forge **tools** for territorial expansion. They cooperatively repair their buildings when a small environmental hazard damages them. The first slice is deliberately limited to prove the authoritative command, simulation, rendering, and synchronization paths.

## Fixed decisions

- The world is procedurally generated and conceptually unbounded. Coordinates are signed 32-bit tile coordinates; generation is deterministic from a world seed.
- Tiles are diamond isometric tiles, 64 by 32 screen pixels, with no elevation in the first slice. Chunks are 16 by 16 tiles; buildings may occupy at most 2 by 2 tiles.
- Logistics uses internal building inventories plus deterministic, recipe-validated links. Completed storage, extractors, and producers send configured-throughput inputs into completed producers or storage buffers; storage-to-storage links are rejected because they would only shuffle items between buffers. Priority ordering reserves source and target capacity per tick, preserves output space for active batches, and reports congestion or recipe reconfiguration. Carriers, roads, belts, and routing remain future work.
- Population uses an aggregated-worker model. A settlement starts with two settlers; completed housing increases its capacity, settlers arrive gradually when capacity is available, and active producers consume deterministic worker slots according to player-set priorities. Satisfaction is derived from shelter, assigned work, and a completed hearth service. Every 200 ticks, healthy settlements gain a settler if they have room; unsatisfied settlements above their protected starting population lose one. Employment, available-worker counts, and actionable housing/work/wellbeing alerts are visible in the HUD. Individual home-to-work walking is deliberately not simulated in the first release; construction-worker ticks and material-delivery state represent required travel and transport, while scouts and threats use explicit bounded paths.
- Every 8 by 8 terrain sector has one deterministic, finite ore deposit and one finite timber grove. The initial chain is ore -> ingot in a smelter, then ingot + wood -> tool in a metallurgy-locked workshop. Tools pay for Territorial Charters. Each player starts with enough wood to build one smelter and can gather more from timber groves.
- Raw extraction is automatable. A mine or lumber camp placed within four tiles of a deposit that still has yield takes one item every four ticks into its own inventory while a settler is assigned to it. Extractors draw from the same finite deposits and the same starting-plot reservation rules as a manual gather command, occupy the shared worker pool alongside recipe producers, and can source logistics links, so a settlement can run ore -> ingot -> tool without a gather click. Placement is rejected when no deposit with remaining yield is in range.
- New players receive a non-overlapping 8 by 8 protected plot on a deterministic spiral around the origin. Owners may build only in their own plot. Every player starts as the owner of a private settlement; owners can invite members, who must accept, revoke membership, and transfer settlement ownership to a current member before leaving. Builders may configure, repair, and deconstruct shared buildings; logistics members may load and withdraw items; ordinary members have no building authority. Direct resource transfers are atomic and retained as participant-visible history.
- Exploration is private until players explicitly cooperate. The server sends each client terrain only for its explored chunks and never sends the deterministic world seed. Territory uses 8 by 8 tile sectors: a player may claim an explored sector adjacent to their existing territory after researching the Territorial Charter. Claimed sectors are public; building inventories and unexplored sectors are private.
- Inactive settlements remain in the world. Simulation runs at the same fidelity for all loaded state in the first slice.
- On planned downtime, simulation pauses at a completed checkpoint. No offline production is granted.
- The first threat is a repairable acid-rain event that damages an owned building; it never targets players or enables PvP.
- The first active PvE threat is a deterministic raider swarm. It spawns away from its selected completed non-center building, navigates through passable terrain, and only damages that fixed target once adjacent. Nearby watchtowers engage it automatically; it never accepts player-issued attack targets, retargets onto a neighbor, or routes through another settlement's protected plot.
- New settlements receive 300 ticks of hazard and raid protection. After 300 ticks without an accepted command, a settlement is treated as inactive: environmental and raider damage can still reduce buildings to 50% health, and at most one raider may pressure that settlement, but unattended damage cannot destroy it. An accepted command immediately makes the settlement active again, so disconnecting is not an instant shield and returning resumes full risk.
- Inactive regions continue through the same deterministic simulation pipeline as observed regions. Level-of-detail simulation is disabled unless a future design and replay test can prove equivalent outcomes; reduced observation must never change production, logistics, hazards, research, or combat results.
- Account deletion is an irreversible authoritative transaction. Players must first transfer every settlement they own; deletion then removes their private simulation state, owned buildings and scouts, land claims, memberships, invitations, block metadata, and display name. Historical transaction, contribution, chat, and moderation records remain bounded and server-side where required for integrity, with chat display-name snapshots replaced. A private tombstone permanently reserves the deleted player identifier and is never sent to clients.
- Administrative investigation uses scoped world, player, settlement, transaction, moderation, and audit inspection rather than direct database editing. Corrections are limited to inventory restoration, settlement-owner repair, retained-message removal, and report resolution. Every accepted correction validates the whole resulting world and atomically stores a completed checkpoint with an immutable actor, reason, operation, target, before/after state, and tick record.
- Commands that overlap while persistence is awaiting I/O are applied through one authoritative queue. Transfer capacity, duplicate retries, role revocation, membership acceptance/removal, and ownership transfer/removal resolve in queue order: each command validates the committed result before it, resources remain conserved, and every settlement retains exactly one owner.
- Every settlement keeps a one-tile westward access lane from its center and a two-tile foreign ownership buffer. Territory claims cannot surround that buffer, buildings cannot block the access lane, and gathering in overlapping starting areas is reserved for the nearest starting plot (with player ID as a deterministic tie-breaker).
- The first global cooperative objective is the Frontier Beacon. Separate settlements contribute a bounded total of tools; contribution commands and settlement/player totals are recorded authoritatively. Completion occurs at an exact deterministic threshold. Every contributor may claim one fixed ingot reward, with the claim command recorded permanently and retries rejected. Clients see global progress and only their own contributor identity and history.
- Settlement owners and builders may reserve up to three shared construction projects on valid settlement land. Any current member can contribute the exact remaining materials. The server retains contributor, item, amount, command, and tick history; once fully funded it creates one building owned by the settlement owner and duplicate retries cannot contribute twice. Project coordinates and contributor history are visible only to relevant settlement members.
- Player and settlement display names are normalized with Unicode NFKC and collapsed whitespace, have bounded type-specific lengths, and are unique under case-insensitive comparison within their respective namespaces. The server applies a configurable moderated-term list before accepting a name.
- The public world directory supports bounded, paginated searches for player and settlement identifiers or display names. Results disclose only names and player IDs, or a settlement's name, ID, owner ID, and member count—never coordinates, territory, inventories, roles, invitations, activity, or hidden-world entities. Directory results can populate resource-transfer and invitation controls.
- Global and settlement chat messages are normalized, length-limited, content-filtered, retained within a fixed bound, and subject to a persisted five-tick sender cooldown. Settlement messages reach only current members. A player's private block list filters prior and future messages from blocked senders, while reports preserve a server-side message snapshot without exposing report metadata to clients.
- Development worlds may be reset freely. Persistent alpha, beta, and public worlds require an announced reset policy.

## Non-goals

This increment does not include production accounts, combat, terrain elevation, roads, belt logistics, trade offers, or mobile support. It targets current desktop Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari at a minimum 1024 by 768 viewport.

## Initial budgets

| Measure                  | Target                                              |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Simulation tick          | 100 ms (10 Hz), below 20 ms for the first slice     |
| Client frame             | below 16.7 ms on the placeholder map                |
| Reconnect                | below 5 seconds on a healthy local network          |
| Protocol message         | 64 KiB maximum                                      |
| Active-player bandwidth  | below 32 KiB/s server-to-client at the target load  |
| Initial players/entities | 20 players / 1,000 entities for the first load test |
